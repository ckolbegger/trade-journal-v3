# D9.5 drafting handoff: the Daily Review redesign

Draft a new deliverable, D9.5: the Daily Review redesign. It covers all the work needed to take the Review page from its current state to everything shown in the coach mockup.

This session is for drafting the deliverable only. Detailed planning and the task breakdown come next, in a later step. Don't draft a task graph, task list or breakdown file now. Focus on getting everything the deliverable must cover called out in it.

## Ask before you draft

Ask me every question you need answered to draft the deliverable well. Ask them one at a time. For each, give your recommendation and the options you'd reject. Don't guess on anything that changes scope, a spec rule, or which deliverable owns a piece of work. If something isn't covered and isn't a big call, record your assumption in the draft and carry on.

## Read first

1. CLAUDE.md, then the "Rules every deliverable follows" and "Recorded decisions" sections of plan/implementation-plan.md.
2. The existing deliverable sections in plan/implementation-plan.md, as models for style and level of detail. Model D9.5 on D4, D5R, D9, D9R2, D10, D11 and D12 in particular. Match their headings: Stories, Capability set, Introduces (Operations, Mechanisms, Seeds, Durable representation), Acceptance, Declared scope, Parallelism, and Open issues where needed.
3. specs/ in full, including specs/glossary.md, specs/design/ and specs/acceptance/. Drafting reads all of specs, not the scoped reading lists.
4. evidence/D09/coach-review-2.md: the coach's review of the current Review page (score 6 of 10, 15 suggestions).
5. prototypes/review-coach-mockup/index.html and prototypes/review-coach-mockup/NOTES.md. These are the target. NOTES.md records how each of the coach's 15 suggestions is addressed, every earlier design rule the mockup breaks, the user's decisions, and what is still open. Open the mockup in a headless browser and click through all seven days; don't rely only on the notes.
6. The current Review page code, enough to know where it starts from: what exists, what changes and what is new. Ground every "this changes" claim in a lookup, not memory.

## What to produce

- A "D9.5" section in plan/implementation-plan.md, placed after D9R2 and before D10, and a row for it in the "Deliverable summary" table.
- User stories that each deliver value on their own, in an order where every story leaves the Review page better and usable. As a guide, there will likely be separate stories for:
  - the day's summary and attention order
  - one Marks step for the whole page, with the far-Mark check and its setting
  - "Plan and now" above the decision, with yesterday's decision remembered
  - the follow-the-Plan rules for Hold and Exit, with reasons
  - recording a sale inside the Review, measured against the Plan's exit
  - expiration and "record later"
  - Replay in the review and the Close Review walk-through
  - plain wording, the meter's sentence and key, and keyboard and focus

  Propose your own split; that list is only a guide.
- For each earlier rule the mockup breaks, say whether it needs a spec change, a recorded decision, or both. List the specs/ sections affected. Don't edit specs/ yet; list the changes as items for me to agree.
- Name the overlaps with later deliverables and propose who owns each one:
  - D12, Stop Discipline: holding past the Stop
  - D17, Revise management: Plans that sell in parts
  - D19, Mark history and change impact: correcting Marks
  - D20, Assignment and Exercise: "record later"
  - D23, Workspace settings: the Settings page
  - D26, Customize Journal prompts: Close Review fields should later come from a journal entry type definition, which isn't specified yet

  Also: I have agreed to move D11 (automatic closing prices) ahead of D10, but that plan change isn't made yet. Say what D9.5 should assume about Marks being typed versus fetched.
- Acceptance: which acceptance scenarios D9.5 makes full or partial, and any new ones it needs.
- Open issues: the questions still open in NOTES.md, plus any you find.

## Style rules

- Plain language first, then the technical detail. Use the app's domain terms from specs/glossary.md. A price is a Mark, never a "close".
- Refer to sections in words, never with the "§" sign.
- Write every ID out ("D10, D11, D12"), never as a range.
- Screen text in the stories should be concise.
- Don't commit anything until I've approved the draft. When I approve it, follow the project's plan-revision rules (plan/revisions.md and the freeze record) for the commit.
