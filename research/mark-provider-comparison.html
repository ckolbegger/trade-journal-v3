Automatic Mark providers compared

Research for D11 (automatic closing prices), written 2026-11-15. Four providers judged against the requirements drawn from D9, D10 and D11 (E*Trade added the same day). Option Marks use the closing bid/ask midpoint (your answer to the requirements' open question Q1).

Recommendation: Marketdata.app, on a paid plan (Starter or higher). It is the only one of the three the app can call straight from the browser with no server, and the only one with past end-of-day bid and ask for option contracts, including expired ones. It meets all three of decision 15's conditions, tested live with your key.

Interactive Brokers and Schwab both need software or a server between the app and the provider. Both lack history for expired options, and both rewrite stock history after splits. E*Trade also needs a server, and gives only prices as of right now: no endpoint takes a date, so it can't fill any past session.

Marketdata.app

Use it, with conditions

Works from the browser (tested live). Gives a midpoint for every past session, including expired contracts. Needs a paid plan, and its terms on deleting data need your judgement.

Interactive Brokers

Not suitable

No browser access for individual accounts. Expired options are never available, and the only credential is your full brokerage login. If you still wanted it, run TWS or IB Gateway on your Mac with a small local bridge.

Schwab / thinkorswim

Not suitable

No historical bid and ask for options, and no data for expired contracts. Signing in needs a server and a manual login every 7 days. thinkorswim on a Mac can't act as a local server.

E*Trade

Not suitable

Current quotes only: no daily bars, no past bid and ask, nothing for expired contracts. No browser access (tested), and the login expires every midnight, so a missed evening loses option Marks for good.

Running something locally or on a server

You asked for these options to be assessed for the three brokers. Both work technically but fall outside the spec's no-server design, so decision 15 says to stop and discuss before choosing either.

Small proxy or serverSoftware on your Mac
Interactive Brokers Needs an always-on server (about $5–20/month); a Cloudflare Worker can't run IBKR's Java gateway. Your brokerage password would live on that server, and automating its login relies on tools IBKR doesn't support. The better of the two. TWS or IB Gateway plus a tiny local bridge. Chrome asks once before a pages.dev page may reach localhost; the gateway uses a self-signed certificate; port 5000 is often taken on macOS.
Schwab / thinkorswim A Cloudflare Worker could hold the app secret and refresh token, but that puts a brokerage token in the cloud, and you'd still log in every 7 days. Not possible with thinkorswim. Its Excel data link (RTD) isn't supported on macOS, DDE is Windows-only, there's no local API, and thinkScript can't write files. Only a manual "Export to file…" of the Position Statement exists.
E*Trade A Cloudflare Pages Function on the app's own address could hold the consumer secret and the day's token, sign quote calls and avoid CORS, for about $0–5 a month. It leaves a token that can trade on an internet-facing server until midnight, needs its own access control, and still needs your login every evening. The data gaps stay. A small script on your Mac (for example with pyetrade) could hold the secret, take the pasted login code each evening, and serve quotes on 127.0.0.1. Free, and the secrets stay off the browser, but it must be kept running, browsers may block or ask before a web page reaches it, and the data gaps stay.

Still to settle before D11's breakdown

  • Marketdata.app's data-deletion term. If a paid plan ends, the terms ask for downloaded data to be deleted, including backups. That conflicts with keeping Marks permanently. Your call.
  • Same-evening option prices on a paid plan. On the Free plan, option prices appear only after 09:30 the next day. Test on a weekday evening during a paid trial.
  • Options that trade until 16:15 (for example SPY, QQQ, IWM): which time the Mark represents.
  • Option and underlying Marks may not be from the same instant. A SPY same-day-expiry chain on 2026-11-13 priced below what the underlying's closing price implied.
  • Not yet tested on Marketdata.app: a rate-limit (429) response body, the 2024 NVDA split (blocked on Free), delisted stocks, and a weekly contract.
  • The requirements' other open questions (splits, whether recovery fetches Bars for days that already have a Mark, credential protection): see the requirements report.
